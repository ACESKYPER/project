ART. 5
Prohibited AI Practices
Applicable from 2 February 2025 · Phase 1

These AI practices are absolutely prohibited under the EU AI Act. Deployment of any system falling within these categories constitutes an infringement subject to the highest penalty tier (up to €35M or 7% global turnover).

PROH-001
Subliminal Manipulation
AI using subliminal techniques beyond conscious perception to materially distort behaviour causing significant harm
Article 5(1)(a)
PROH-002
Exploitation of Vulnerabilities
AI exploiting vulnerabilities linked to age, disability, or socioeconomic status to distort behaviour causing significant harm
Article 5(1)(b)
PROH-003
Social Scoring by Public Authorities
AI systems used by public authorities for social scoring leading to detrimental treatment of persons or groups
Article 5(1)(c)
PROH-004
Real-Time Remote Biometric ID
Real-time remote biometric identification in publicly accessible spaces for law enforcement (narrow exceptions apply)
Article 5(1)(d)
PROH-005
Workplace Emotion Recognition
AI inferring emotions of natural persons in workplace or educational settings (except for safety or medical purposes)
Article 5(1)(f)
PROH-006
Biometric Categorisation by Sensitive Attributes
AI categorising individuals by race, political opinions, religious beliefs, sexual orientation from biometric data
Article 5(1)(g)
PROH-007
Untargeted Facial Recognition Scraping
AI creating or expanding facial recognition databases through untargeted scraping from the internet or CCTV footage
Article 5(1)(e)
ANNEX III
High-Risk AI Classification
Article 6 + Annex III · 8 Categories

AI systems falling within the following Annex III categories are presumed high-risk and subject to Title III Chapter 2 requirements, unless the provider demonstrates via a documented assessment that they pose no significant risk to health, safety, or fundamental rights.

HR-CAT-01 · Annex III, Item 1
Biometric Identification & Categorisation
  • Remote biometric identification systems (post-remote)
  • Biometric categorisation inferring sensitive attributes
  • Emotion recognition systems (where not prohibited)
HR-CAT-02 · Annex III, Item 2
Critical Infrastructure
  • Safety components in critical digital infrastructure
  • Road traffic management safety components
  • Water, gas, heating, electricity supply safety components
HR-CAT-03 · Annex III, Item 3
Education & Vocational Training
  • Admission or access to educational institutions
  • Evaluation of learning outcomes
  • Assessment and evaluation of students
  • Detection of prohibited behaviour during tests
HR-CAT-04 · Annex III, Item 4
Employment & Workers Management
  • Recruitment and selection of natural persons
  • Promotion and termination decisions
  • Task allocation and performance monitoring
HR-CAT-05 · Annex III, Item 5
Access to Essential Services
  • Creditworthiness assessment and credit scoring
  • Risk assessment in life and health insurance
  • Emergency call evaluation and classification
  • Public benefit eligibility assessment
HR-CAT-06 · Annex III, Item 6
Law Enforcement
  • Criminal risk assessment of natural persons
  • Polygraph and similar tools
  • Evidence reliability assessment
  • Predictive policing systems
  • Criminal investigation profiling
HR-CAT-07 · Annex III, Item 7
Migration & Border Control
  • Border control risk assessment tools
  • Asylum, visa, residence permit examination
  • Detection and identification in migration context
HR-CAT-08 · Annex III, Item 8
Administration of Justice
  • Judicial research and interpretation assistance
  • Electoral and voting behaviour influence
  • Dispute resolution systems
ART. 8–15
Mandatory Requirements
Title III, Chapter 2 · Applicable from 2 August 2026

All high-risk AI systems must satisfy the following seven requirements before being placed on the EU market or put into service. Click each requirement to expand obligations and evidence requirements.

REQ-001 Risk Management System Article 9

Providers must establish, implement, document and maintain a risk management system throughout the entire lifecycle of the high-risk AI system. The system must be iterative — it runs throughout the entire system lifetime and is regularly updated.

Obligations & Evidence Requirements
Identify and analyse all known and reasonably foreseeable risks associated with the system Risk register documentation
Estimate and evaluate risks during intended use and reasonably foreseeable misuse Risk assessment reports
Evaluate risks based on post-market monitoring data and adopt suitable measures Post-market monitoring records & treatment documentation
Test and validate the AI system to identify appropriate risk management measures Testing protocols & validation reports
RMS-DOCUMENTED RMS-LIFECYCLE-MAINTAINED RMS-VALIDATED
REQ-002 Data and Data Governance Article 10

High-risk AI systems using training, validation or testing data must be subject to data governance and management practices addressing design choices, data collection, preparation, and examination for biases.

Obligations & Evidence Requirements
Data governance practices covering design choices, collection, preparation, and bias examination Data governance policy & dataset documentation
Datasets must be relevant, sufficiently representative, and to the best extent possible free of errors Dataset quality assessment reports
Appropriate statistical properties including as regards persons or groups the AI is used on Statistical representativeness analysis
Special category data used only where strictly necessary with appropriate safeguards DPIA & processing records
DATA-GOVERNED DATA-REPRESENTATIVE DATA-BIAS-EXAMINED
REQ-003 Technical Documentation Article 11 + Annex IV

Providers must draw up technical documentation before placing the high-risk AI system on the market and keep it updated throughout the system lifecycle. Documentation must contain all information necessary for competent authorities to assess compliance.

Obligations & Evidence Requirements
Technical documentation drawn up before market placement and kept current Technical documentation file (Annex IV compliant)
Contains all information necessary for competent authority assessment Annex IV checklist completion
SMEs may use simplified documentation requirements where applicable Simplified documentation (SME declaration)
TECHDOC-COMPLETE TECHDOC-CURRENT TECHDOC-ANNEXIV-COMPLIANT
REQ-004 Record-Keeping and Automatic Logging Article 12

High-risk AI systems must be designed and developed with capabilities enabling automatic recording of events throughout their lifetime, to enable post-market monitoring and risk detection.

Obligations & Evidence Requirements
Automatic logging capabilities built into system architecture throughout lifetime System logging architecture documentation
Logs enable monitoring of operation and detection of situations resulting in risk Log format specification & retention policy
Logging integrated with post-market monitoring system Post-market monitoring integration specification
Law enforcement systems: logs retained for minimum periods per applicable law Retention schedule & deletion records
LOGGING-AUTOMATIC LOGGING-COMPLETE LOGGING-RETAINED
REQ-005 Transparency and Information to Deployers Article 13

High-risk AI systems must be designed to ensure sufficient transparency enabling deployers to interpret outputs and use systems appropriately. Comprehensive instructions for use are mandatory.

Obligations & Evidence Requirements
Instructions for use in digital format — concise, complete, correct, and clear Instructions for use document
Instructions specify provider identity, system characteristics, performance, limitations, and conditions of use Article 13(3) checklist completion
Declared accuracy levels and known/foreseeable risk circumstances documented Accuracy specifications & risk disclosure
Human oversight measures and computational resource requirements specified Human oversight specification
TRANSPARENCY-DOCUMENTED INSTRUCTIONS-COMPLETE INSTRUCTIONS-ACCESSIBLE
REQ-006 Human Oversight Article 14

High-risk AI systems must be designed with appropriate human-machine interface tools enabling effective oversight during use, including the ability to override, correct, or halt the system.

Obligations & Evidence Requirements
System designed to be effectively overseen by natural persons during the period of use Human oversight design specification
Oversight-assigned persons can understand capabilities and limitations fully Oversight training materials & competency framework
Oversight persons can detect anomalies, dysfunctions and unexpected performance Anomaly detection interface specification
Oversight persons can disregard, override or reverse system output Override capability documentation
Oversight persons can interrupt system operation via stop button or equivalent Stop/interrupt mechanism specification
HUMAN-OVERSIGHT-DESIGNED OVERRIDE-CAPABLE INTERRUPT-CAPABLE
REQ-007 Accuracy, Robustness and Cybersecurity Article 15

High-risk AI systems must achieve appropriate levels of accuracy, robustness, and cybersecurity throughout their lifecycle, with declared metrics and protection against adversarial attacks.

Obligations & Evidence Requirements
Declared accuracy level achievable and documented in instructions for use Accuracy metrics & benchmark documentation
Resilient against errors, faults, or inconsistencies within system or environment Robustness testing reports
Resilient against adversarial manipulation including data poisoning and adversarial examples Adversarial robustness assessment
Technical redundancy and fail-safe solutions where appropriate Redundancy & failsafe architecture documentation
Protected against cybersecurity risks appropriate to the intended use context Cybersecurity assessment & penetration testing records
ACCURACY-DECLARED ROBUSTNESS-TESTED CYBERSECURITY-ASSESSED
ART. 16–73
Provider Obligations
Title III, Chapter 3 · Articles 16–27 + 47, 49, 72, 73

Beyond the seven technical requirements, providers bear the following procedural and administrative obligations before and after placing a high-risk AI system on the market.

PROV-OBL-001 Quality Management System Article 17

Providers must put a quality management system in place ensuring compliance. The QMS must cover: regulatory compliance strategy, design and development procedures, post-market monitoring, incident reporting, and record-keeping procedures.

QMS-ESTABLISHED QMS-DOCUMENTED QMS-MAINTAINED
PROV-OBL-002 EU Declaration of Conformity Article 47

Providers must draw up an EU Declaration of Conformity before placing the system on the market. The declaration must identify the provider, describe the system, reference applicable harmonised standards, and name any notified body involved.

DoC-PREPARED DoC-SIGNED DoC-CURRENT
PROV-OBL-003 EU Database Registration Article 49

Providers must register the high-risk AI system in the EU AI database before placing it on the market. The database is publicly accessible for most high-risk categories, with limited-access provisions for law enforcement and border control systems.

DB-REGISTERED DB-ID-ASSIGNED
PROV-OBL-004 Serious Incident Reporting Article 73

Providers must report serious incidents to relevant market surveillance authorities within defined timeframes.

Reporting Timelines
Immediately life-threatening or fatal incident Within 72 hours
Serious incident (non-fatal) Within 15 days
Death of a person causally linked to AI system Within 10 days
INCIDENT-REPORTING-ACTIVE TIMELINE-COMPLIANT
TITLE V
General-Purpose AI Models
Articles 51–56 · Applicable from 2 August 2025

GPAI models face a two-tier obligation structure based on training compute. Systemic risk models face additional obligations including mandatory red-teaming.

GPAI · STANDARD TIER
Standard GPAI Models
Threshold: Training compute > 10²³ FLOPs
Technical documentation (Annex XI) · Information to downstream providers · Copyright compliance policy · Training data summary publication
GPAI · SYSTEMIC RISK TIER
Systemic Risk Models
Threshold: Training compute > 10²⁵ FLOPs or Commission designation
All standard obligations plus: adversarial testing / red-teaming · serious incident reporting to Commission · cybersecurity protection measures · energy efficiency reporting
ART. 43
Conformity Assessment Routes
Article 43 · Two Available Routes
CA-ROUTE-01 · Annex VI
Internal Control
Provider conducts conformity assessment internally against applicable requirements. Available where harmonised standards are fully applied.
Applicable to: HR-CAT-02, 03, 04, 05, 07, 08
CA-ROUTE-02 · Annex VII
Third-Party Assessment
Assessment by EU-designated Notified Body. Mandatory for biometric identification and law enforcement AI where harmonised standards are not fully applied.
Mandatory for: HR-CAT-01, HR-CAT-06
ART. 99
Administrative Penalty Structure
Article 99 · Three Tiers
Violation Category Maximum Administrative Fine
Prohibited AI practices (Article 5 violations) €35,000,000 or 7% global annual turnover
Whichever is higher
Non-compliance with high-risk AI requirements or GPAI obligations €15,000,000 or 3% global annual turnover
Whichever is higher
Incorrect, incomplete or misleading information to notified bodies or authorities €7,500,000 or 1.5% global annual turnover
Whichever is higher. For SMEs: lower of absolute or percentage cap applies
