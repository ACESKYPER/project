GIAS Institute has published its inaugural standard: a structured, machine-readable compliance schema for the EU Artificial Intelligence Act. The schema maps every prohibited practice, all eight Annex III high-risk categories, seven mandatory technical requirements, provider obligations, and the full penalty structure — in a format that AI systems can query directly.

This is the technical proof of GIAS's function. Standards bodies do not only govern — they produce. This schema is freely available, openly licensed, and designed to be integrated into compliance tooling, regulatory submissions, and conformity assessment systems worldwide.

Our Mandate

Built for
permanence,
not product.

GIAS Institute is constituted as a permanent institution — not a technology company, not a compliance tool, not a consultancy. Its mandate is the independent development of international standards for artificial intelligence: standards that derive their authority from technical rigour and institutional independence, not from commercial relationships or political alignment.

The global AI industry requires trust infrastructure in the same way that the internet required ICANN and global finance required SWIFT. GIAS Institute exists to build and operate that infrastructure — permanently, without capture, for the benefit of all jurisdictions.

01
Standards Development
International standards for the design, deployment, audit, and certification of AI systems — developed through open, multi-stakeholder processes with public comment periods and documented rationale.
02
Certification Infrastructure
Technical frameworks through which AI systems, developers, and deployers demonstrate compliance with applicable standards — across jurisdictions and regulatory frameworks simultaneously.
03
Regulatory Engagement
Active engagement with national and supranational regulatory bodies — EU, African Union, ASEAN, and beyond — to ensure GIAS standards inform and interoperate with the full spectrum of AI governance frameworks.
Governance

GIAS Institute is being constituted as a Swiss association under Articles 60 to 79 of the Swiss Civil Code, with its registered seat in Geneva, Canton of Geneva, Switzerland. Its governance structure comprises a General Assembly, a Board of Governors, and a Technical Standards Council.

The Institute operates under a Mission Lock Provision and a Non-Capture Clause — structural protections ensuring that no single commercial, governmental, or geopolitical interest may control or unduly influence its standard-setting function. No member or affiliated group may hold more than one quarter of voting rights regardless of membership volume.

Legal Basis Swiss Civil Code, Articles 60–79. Permanent seat: Geneva, Switzerland.
Membership Categories Founding Members, Institutional Members (governments, regulators, standards bodies), Associate Members, and Observer Members.
Non-Distribution No part of the Institute's revenue or assets may be distributed to members or officers. All surplus is applied exclusively to the Mission.
Standards Procedures All standards development processes include mandatory public comment periods of not less than sixty days, appeals mechanisms, and documented rationale.
